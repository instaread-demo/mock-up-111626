Why We Actually Use Strava

The simplest reason is that Strava puts all of our running in one place. It syncs with GPS watches and devices from brands like Garmin, COROS and Apple, so whichever one a runner has on their wrist, every workout ends up in a single feed. If you’re still choosing a watch, our guide to the best running watches covers the options. For a team that tests a lot of different kit, having everything in one feed makes life much easier.

Tracking Shoe Mileage

This is the feature we rely on most. Strava lets you assign each run to a specific pair of shoes and keeps a running total of the miles on them. When we’re testing a shoe we can see exactly how far we’ve run in it, which helps us judge how it’s holding up and means our long-term verdicts are based on real numbers rather than guesswork.

It’s just as handy if you simply want to know when your daily trainers are due for replacing. If you’re after a new pair, our list of the best running shoes is a good place to start.

A Training Log That Does The Work For You

Strava tracks the numbers we care about, including pace, distance, elevation, heart rate and power, and it keeps hold of all of it. Whether you keep your activities private or share them publicly, it works as a long-term training diary, so you can look back over weeks and months to see how your fitness is progressing, review old sessions and spot trends you’d never notice from one run to the next.

Segments And Leaderboards

Segments are stretches of road or trail that Strava tracks automatically using GPS. Every time you run one, you can compare your time with your own previous efforts, or see how you stack up against everyone else who’s covered the same ground. If you’re competitive, the ultimate prize is a KOM, QOM or CR (King of the Mountain, Queen of the Mountain or Course Record), the title for the fastest recorded time on a segment. Even if you’re not chasing one, it’s a fun way to turn a regular route into a mini time trial.

Finding New Routes

Strava’s heatmaps show where other runners and cyclists actually go, which makes it easy to find good routes in an unfamiliar area. Following other local athletes is a good way to spot new ones near home as well. We use it whenever we’re travelling and want to get a run in without spending an hour planning.

Challenges, Clubs And Community

Strava runs virtual challenges every month, covering things like distance, elevation and race pace, which are a good way to stay accountable when motivation is low. You can also join running clubs, cycling groups or your own set of friends, and seeing what everyone else is up to is a great source of camaraderie (and occasionally a little healthy pressure to get out of the door).

Staying Safe

Strava’s Beacon feature shares your real-time GPS location with chosen emergency contacts while you’re out on a run. If you’re heading out alone on an unfamiliar route or running in the dark, it’s a simple way to give someone peace of mind.
