Helen Haines discharged from hospital four weeks after attack

Rob Harris of SMH|Sydney Morning Herald
November 15, 2026 • 11:19pm

Independent MP Helen Haines has been discharged from hospital almost a month after an alleged targeted attack outside her electorate office left her fighting for life and resulted in the amputation of both legs.

The 65-year-old federal MP for Indi has spent four weeks recovering at Melbourne’s Alfred Hospital after she was repeatedly struck by a four-wheel-drive outside her Wangaratta office on October 19.

In her first public statement since leaving hospital, Haines confirmed she had entered the next stage of her recovery but would remain in Melbourne for the foreseeable future.

“This is a really positive moment, and I am full of gratitude for the expert and compassionate care I received at the Alfred and am receiving in this next stage,” she said.

“I appreciate the thousands of warm wishes and supportive messages from people across Indi, throughout Australia and around the world. They are giving me strength as I focus on this next phase of healing.”

AD

Haines remains on leave from federal parliament until at least the end of the parliamentary year, with the Albanese government pledging to accommodate her recovery for as long as necessary.

Haines, a former nurse and rural health researcher, has represented the north-east Victorian electorate of Indi since 2019 and was re-elected for a third term in May last year.

Her electorate offices in Wangaratta and Wodonga reopened late last month and continue to provide services to constituents. Staff are operating the offices three days a week, from Tuesday to Thursday, with assistance available by email outside those hours.

Victorian man Brendan Nugent, 43, has been charged with dangerous driving causing serious injury, intentionally causing serious injury and the Commonwealth offence of causing harm to a public official.

Police allege the attack was deliberate and targeted. Nugent remains in custody and has not entered a plea. His case is scheduled to return to Wangaratta Magistrates’ Court on February 4.

Haines’ husband, Phil, described his wife as a fighter in the days after the attack, saying she was determined to recover with the support of her family.

The attack prompted an extraordinary outpouring of sympathy across the political divide and renewed concerns about the safety of elected representatives.

Parliament paid tribute to Haines when it resumed in October, with fellow independent Zali Steggall placing an orange cardboard cockatoo on her empty seat in the House of Representatives.

A fundraising appeal for Save the Children, nominated by Haines’ family in lieu of flowers, has raised more than $1.4 million.

“I am overwhelmed by the generosity of Australians in the form of donations to Save the Children and I look forward to working with them in due course on applying these funds to improve the lives of children,” Haines said.

Prime Minister Anthony Albanese, travelling in the Philippines, said in a statement he had been in regular contact with Haines and her “extraordinary family”.

“All our thoughts and prayers continue to be with Helen for her recover,” he said. “We continue to provide support to Helen, her family, community and staff as she undertakes her rehabilitation.”

-Sydney Morning Herald
